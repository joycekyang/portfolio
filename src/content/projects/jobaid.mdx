## Overview & Learning Objectives

This is part two of a two-part Lesson Series focusing on Ozobot Robotics in grades 3–5. The first lesson focuses on analog coloring techniques to code the robot. This second part uses digital programming to control the robot using the software **OzoBlockly**.

Clarified procedural confusion, specifically regarding the "flashing" (loading) and "calibration" processes, which are the most common points of failure for new Ozobot users. By reducing the instructions to six visual steps, I eliminated the need for lengthy verbal explanations and repetitive troubleshooting questions, allowing the instructor to focus on coding logic rather than technical setup.

* **Focus:** Computing Systems and Hardware/Software Integration
* **1B-CS-02:** Students will model how computer hardware and software work together to accomplish tasks. *(Evidence: Mastering the three-step digital-to-physical synchronization: creating digital logic, calibrating sensors, and flash-loading code.)*
* **1B-AP-10:** Students will construct a multi-step digital program by sequencing drag-and-drop command blocks within the OzoBlockly workspace.
* **1B-CS-03:** Students will determine system-level problems (troubleshooting) by interpreting visual feedback from the hardware. *(Evidence: Using the "flickering green" light or the calibration color-cycle to verify successful code transmission.)*

## Process

I utilized a multimodal capture approach to bridge the gap between digital software and physical hardware:

* **Digital Capture:** Used TechSmith Snagit to capture high-resolution screenshots of the OzoBlockly interface, ensuring UI elements (buttons and menus) were crisp and recognizable.
* **Physical Capture:** Photographed the physical interactions (hand placement and robot positioning against the screen). This is a critical instructional choice, as it shows the learner exactly how to hold the device, a nuance often lost in digital-only guides.
* **Instructional Signaling:** Applied signaling principles using high-contrast red numbered callouts and directional arrows, guiding the learner’s eye through the "Z-pattern" of the layout so they don't skip critical steps like calibration (Step 4).
* **Concise Micro-Copy:** Distilled complex technical steps into clear Action Verbs (*Open*, *Create*, *Flash*, *Calibrate*, *Run*) to minimize reading time and maximize active "doing."
* **Accessibility Integration:** Integrated a QR Code to allow students to bypass manual URL entry, reducing the barrier to entry for younger learners.

## Takeaway

This project demonstrates my proficiency in visual problem-solving and just-in-time performance support. Since implementing this job aid across **72 separate lessons reaching 1,800+ students**, I observed an **80% reduction in technical troubleshooting requests** during the transition from digital coding to physical hardware execution.

By pairing crisp Snagit screenshots of the OzoBlockly interface with real-world photography of hand placement, I created a high-fidelity "bridge" for the learner, resulting in a low-friction learning environment where students can focus 100% of their cognitive load on computational logic.
